## Overall Impression

YellowStorm has a credible enterprise story hiding in plain sight: governed Credit Risk knowledge, a conversational entry point, and repeatable playbooks. The interface currently asks users to assemble that story themselves. The single biggest opportunity is to replace feature-first navigation with an explicit governed work journey: understand the business context, ask with evidence, operationalize the result, and monitor control.

## What's Working

1. **Governance feels specific and credible.** Readiness, publication state, ownership, and dry-run signals communicate control rather than generic AI decoration.
2. **Home exposes the ecosystem.** Conversation, workspace context, recent playbooks, and governed scopes appear together, giving the right raw material for an integrated experience.
3. **The responsive shell is viable.** At 390x844, the composer remained usable and navigation moved into a named, keyboard-focusable dialog.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Backend failure masquerades as an empty Semantic Models collection

**Why it matters:** Two `GET /api/v1/semantic-models` requests returned HTTP 500, while the UI invited the user to create a first business map. A CxO can reasonably conclude that company knowledge does not exist; an analyst cannot diagnose or recover.

**Fix:** Implement distinct loading, empty, stale-data, and error states. Preserve cached data, explain the failure in plain language, expose Retry, and provide a support-safe reference without leaking internals.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The governed value chain is invisible

**Why it matters:** The strongest Credit Risk scope does not lead naturally to a scoped question, cited answer, or reusable playbook. Users must return to global navigation and remember context, which destroys the one-minute story.

**Fix:** Make scope/model context persistent and add explicit next actions: `Ask with this scope`, `Review evidence`, and `Turn into a playbook`. Show the active scope and governance status throughout the journey.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Playbooks and demo data communicate operational risk, not confidence

**Why it matters:** Duplicate, failed, test, typo-heavy, and unclear entries sit beside numerous equal-weight actions. Executives see unfinished operations; business users fear deleting or editing the wrong item.

**Fix:** Create a curated demo tenant/view with 3-5 credible business playbooks, one polished Credit Risk hero flow, clean status history, and secondary row actions consolidated into one overflow menu.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] Navigation is organized by implementation, not user outcome

**Why it matters:** More than ten peer destinations mix daily work, platform structure, governance, administration, integrations, projects, and beta features. Each persona must learn the system map before doing useful work.

**Fix:** Group destinations by outcome and role. Promote a short Operate path such as `Ask`, `Knowledge`, `Automations`, and `Governance`; move Teams, Groups, Apps, Marketplace, Admin, and beta areas behind clearly labeled secondary groups or role-aware navigation.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] Language, visual density, and component treatment weaken trust

**Why it matters:** French and English mix across shell and modules; technical depth varies by label; Playbooks and Workspace stack many nested surfaces and small/low-contrast text. The detector also found layout-property animation in global and Playbook panels.

**Fix:** Enforce one locale per session, normalize business terminology, establish one primary action per surface, reduce nested containers, raise compact text to accessible sizes/contrast, and replace width transitions with transform/opacity where appropriate.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Emotional Journey

Arrival creates curiosity through an approachable conversational prompt. Orientation quickly becomes overload because the product boundary and priority are unclear. Workspace and Governance rebuild confidence with real enterprise structure. The silent Semantic Models failure introduces doubt, and the Playbooks beta warning plus noisy records turn automation into perceived risk. The ending is fragmented: the user can see all ingredients of a compelling governed-agent story but never receives the payoff as one continuous flow.

## Persona Red Flags

**CxO:** The first minute exposes product nouns rather than a business outcome. Failed/test playbooks and a false empty knowledge state undermine confidence in governance and readiness.

**Data Analyst:** Semantic Models lacks visible source coverage, lineage, freshness, and health diagnostics. A failed collection request appears indistinguishable from zero models, and context does not carry into a conversation or playbook.

**Business user:** The sidebar contains too many unfamiliar destinations; labels shift between French, English, business, and technical language; routine View/Edit actions sit beside destructive and integration actions with similar visual weight.

## One-Minute Demo Test

The current product is not yet reliable enough for a polished one-minute promo because the intended journey is not explicit and Semantic Models can misrepresent failure as absence. The credible target story is: open the published `Credit Risk` governed scope, ask which accounts require attention, show a concise cited answer with governance status, convert it into a `Watchlist Prioritization` playbook, run it, and end on an approval/audit result. That sequence demonstrates knowledge, agents, automation, and governance without touring five disconnected modules.
